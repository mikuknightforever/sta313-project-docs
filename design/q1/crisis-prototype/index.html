Read this first What the measures mean

A case is not a person. Caseloads count administrative units receiving assistance. They do not measure all people in need or people unable to access a program.

Unemployment is context. It captures one part of economic pressure. OW and ODSP have different eligibility rules and populations; unemployment is particularly incomplete as a measure of disability-related needs.

Different evidence, different scope. The first two figures use Ontario-wide quarterly data. Figure 3 uses annual illustrative household incomes and a Toronto poverty threshold.

Enough to live on?

Count the support. Compare it with the need.

Receiving assistance and receiving enough assistance are different questions. This annual example compares total welfare income with a Toronto Market Basket Measure (MBM) poverty threshold.

100% = the reported poverty threshold

Figure 3. Annual total welfare income ÷ annual MBM threshold × 100. A value below 100% means income falls below this benchmark for the specified example. It does not estimate the share of residents who are covered.

A deliberately specific comparison

One adult. Two program examples.

Total welfare income can include tax credits and one-time payments, as specified by the source. It is not simply the monthly OW or ODSP payment multiplied by twelve.

These are annual illustrative scenarios, not observed incomes for every recipient. They do not capture all costs, access barriers, or disability-related needs.

The data do not establish

Worth investigating

How regular assistance and temporary benefits interacted, who remained outside support, and how disability-related costs change the adequacy assessment.
