The OW–ODSP gap on the map, quarter by quarter, and where it could be by 2030

Loading data…

Motivation. Policy analysts look across the province at once and ask what comes next. A map that plays through 93 quarters shows every CMA's gap together; the outlook extends it to 2030 with three simple rules whose past accuracy is shown next to them.

Appeal. Press Play and Ontario's CMAs fade from dark to light as their gaps shrink; at 2026 Q1 the map turns hatched and steps into the outlook.

Cost. Colour is read less precisely than position, so values are printed on the map and in the card; large CMAs dominate a map, so equal tiles are one click away; a projection is not a forecast, and the rules disagree for some CMAs.

This version does not use the agreed dumbbell baseline, and it uses the filled map (choropleth) that the other Q2 versions avoided because CMA areas differ about tenfold. Here the map is kept for its familiarity, the equal-tile view removes the area effect, and the exact values sit in the card, Figure 2 and Table 2.

Read this first: what the numbers measure

Gap

ODSP share − OW share of cases that are a single person without children, in percentage points (pp), for one CMA in one quarter. Darker = wider gap.

Outlook

2026 Q2 – 2030 Q4, hatched. Each rule extends a CMA's gap mechanically from the data up to 2026 Q1. It is a projection, not a forecast: it knows nothing about future policy or the economy.

The three rules

Convergence: the gap keeps moving toward the middle CMA's gap at the rate seen in the past. No change: it stays at its 2026 Q1 value. 10-year trend: it follows its straight-line trend of the last 10 years.

How the rules were tested

Each rule was run from every past quarter that has 10 years of data before it, using only the data up to that quarter, and compared with what happened. The "likely range" is the middle 80% of those past misses.

Cases, small CMAs, pandemic

A case can include more than one person. Shares in small CMAs move more from quarter to quarter. Pandemic: March 2020 – May 2023 (2020 Q1 – 2023 Q1 in quarterly data).
