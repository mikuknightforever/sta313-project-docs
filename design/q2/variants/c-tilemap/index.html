Motivation. Analysts look for regions worth a closer look. Equal tiles in rough map positions keep geography without letting Toronto's or Thunder Bay's land area dominate, and each tile carries the full 2003–2026 record. The waterfall answers "when" for one CMA.

Appeal. Ontario drawn as sixteen small panels, all with columns shrinking from left to right. The waterfall's single upward step for a CMA that widened during the pandemic is easy to remember.

Cost. Small panels give less precision than one large chart; tile positions are approximate (Thunder Bay is far farther north-west than shown). It is a second map on the site next to Part 3's map, and it costs the most to build.

This version does not use the agreed dumbbell baseline, because a ranked list removes geography and shows only two moments in time. Here every CMA keeps its approximate place and its full time series; the ranking is still available ("Ranked" arrangement and Table 1), and the waterfall replaces the dual lines with an explicit before / during / after breakdown.

Read this first: what the numbers measure

Share of cases

Single-person cases without children ÷ all cases × 100, for one program, one CMA, one quarter. It describes who is on the caseload, not how many single residents receive assistance.

Gap

ODSP share − OW share, in percentage points (pp). ODSP's share is higher in every CMA and every quarter, so every column runs from the OW share up to the ODSP share.

Narrowed by

Gap at the start quarter − gap at the end quarter (default 2003 Q1 → 2026 Q1). Positive means narrower. The waterfall splits it at 2019 Q4 and 2023 Q1, the last quarters before and during the pandemic.

Cases, small CMAs, pandemic

A case can include more than one person. Shares in small CMAs move more from quarter to quarter, so hover shows the counts. Pandemic: March 2020 – May 2023 (2020 Q1 – 2023 Q1 in quarterly data).
