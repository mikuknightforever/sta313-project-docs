Two clouds: OW and ODSP single-person shares in 15 CMAs

Loading data…

Motivation. One picture should carry the main result for every CMA at once: how both shares moved, which program moved, and how far apart the programs are. Each dot is one CMA in one year, so nothing is averaged away.

Appeal. A blue cloud climbing toward an almost flat orange one, with the empty space between them closing. Hovering a dot zips that CMA's two dots together; the teeth get shorter year by year.

Cost. Readers must learn that sideways position inside a year means nothing; the yearly columns use the first quarter only; 24 columns are tight on a phone. The space between the clouds is not any one CMA's gap, so the page has to say so.

This version does not use the agreed dumbbell baseline, because a dumbbell shows two moments and only the size of the gap; it cannot show that the OW share rose in every CMA while the ODSP share barely moved. The ranking is kept in Figure 2 (start and end gaps, slopes) and Table 1.

Read this first: what the numbers measure

Share of cases

Single-person cases without children ÷ all cases × 100, for one program, one CMA, one quarter. It describes who is on the caseload, not how many single residents receive assistance.

Gap

ODSP share − OW share, in percentage points (pp), for one CMA. In Figure 1 it is the length of a zipper tooth: the line joining a CMA's blue and orange dots in the same year.

Space between the clouds

The lowest ODSP share among the 15 CMAs minus the highest OW share in the same quarter. It compares the two groups of CMAs, so it is smaller than every single CMA's gap.

Narrowed by

Gap at the start quarter − gap at the end quarter (default 2003 Q1 → 2026 Q1). Positive means narrower; it does not mean narrower every quarter.

Cases, small CMAs, pandemic

A case can include more than one person. Shares in small CMAs move more from quarter to quarter, so hover shows the counts. Pandemic: March 2020 – May 2023 (2020 Q1 – 2023 Q1 in quarterly data).
