Motivation. The "where" question is a ranking task, and a ranked dumbbell puts every CMA's start and end gap on one common scale. The linked lines then answer "when, and which program".

Appeal. One glance: every end dot sits left of its start dot. The sorted list is easy to drop into a briefing.

Cost. Familiar rather than novel. It drops geography, and the dumbbell shows the size of the gap but not which program moved; that needs the second chart.

Read this first: what the numbers measure

Share of cases

Single-person cases without children ÷ all cases × 100, for one program, one CMA, one quarter. It describes who is on the caseload, not how many single residents receive assistance.

Gap

ODSP share − OW share, in percentage points (pp). ODSP's share is higher in every CMA and every quarter, so the gap is always positive and never reverses.

Narrowed by

Gap at the start quarter − gap at the end quarter of the period you choose (default 2003 Q1 → 2026 Q1). Positive means narrower. Smaller at the end does not mean smaller every quarter.

Cases, small CMAs, pandemic

A case can include more than one person. Shares in small CMAs move more from quarter to quarter, so hover shows the counts. Pandemic: March 2020 – May 2023 (2020 Q1 – 2023 Q1 in quarterly data).
