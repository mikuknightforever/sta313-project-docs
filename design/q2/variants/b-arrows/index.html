The gap narrowed everywhere. Which program moved?

Loading data…

Motivation. A gap can shrink when one share rises or when the other falls. Placing each CMA in OW-share × ODSP-share space shows both shares and the gap in one mark, and the decomposition bars say how much each program contributed.

Appeal. Fifteen arrows all running toward the "equal shares" line, almost flat: an image that says "OW moved, ODSP barely did" without a sentence.

Cost. Less familiar: readers must learn the diagonal reference lines. The time path between start and end is only seen by moving the slider, and arrows overlap in the crowded middle.

This version does not use the agreed dumbbell baseline, because a dumbbell shows only the size of the gap; it cannot show whether the OW share rose or the ODSP share fell. Here the arrow's direction and the split bars answer "which program" directly, while the ranking is kept in Figure 2.

Read this first: what the numbers measure

Share of cases

Single-person cases without children ÷ all cases × 100, for one program, one CMA, one quarter. It describes who is on the caseload, not how many single residents receive assistance.

Gap

ODSP share − OW share, in percentage points (pp). ODSP's share is higher in every CMA and every quarter, so every arrow stays above the "equal shares" line.

Narrowed by

Gap at the start quarter − gap at the end quarter (default 2003 Q1 → 2026 Q1) = OW share change − ODSP share change. Positive means narrower; it does not mean narrower every quarter.

Cases, small CMAs, pandemic

A case can include more than one person. Shares in small CMAs move more from quarter to quarter, so hover shows the counts. Pandemic: March 2020 – May 2023 (2020 Q1 – 2023 Q1 in quarterly data).
